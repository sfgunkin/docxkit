Not a defect list. `BACKLOG.md` is where defects go, and everything here
that belongs there is marked **→ BACKLOG**. This is the shape of the
package: what is coupled to what, what is inferred where it should be
recorded, and what the growth of the last month did to both.

six findings are all closed and each of which is now a gate. Nothing

**Measured on** commit `6ef6eca`, Python 3.14, this machine.
**Tools run:** AST walks over 59 modules, griffe 2.x (`api_check`),
vulture 2.x, radon 6.x, `-X importtime` in fresh interpreters, an import

**Scale:** 59 modules, 33,154 source lines (38 % prose: 26 % docstrings,
12 % comments), 73,703 test lines in 125 files, 5,494 tests in ~27 s,
15,209 lines of markdown. 936 commits in 34 days, two authors.
Downstream: 274 paper scripts across nine manuscripts, from an editable
install, at the tip.

**Verdict.** The bones are sound and the design is coherent — a function
library over three raw representations, a regex kernel, and a gate
culture that is the compensating control for extreme velocity. The
structural risk sat in three places: a kernel private by name and public
by use; a workflow engine whose state is inferred from filesystem
artifacts; and a prose-as-memory bet whose claims have a half-life.

## 1. `revision` infers state from artifacts → BACKLOG (partly)

`revision/` is 14 halves and 53 public names — a workflow engine. Its
state is `prev.docx` + `batch.docx` + a hash stamp + `redlines/` +
`rescue/` + `paper.toml` + `log.md`. Nothing records *what happened*;
every reader reconstructs it from which files exist and what they hash
to.

Two of the last three S2 defects are inference bugs of that shape:

* the verdict bug closed in `39fe472` could only distinguish "rejected
  in full" from "never promoted" by finding `promote`'s redline copy —
  the only trace a batch ever touched the paper. The two leave the
  manuscript **identical**;
* the open S2 (`build` silent on a pending working file) is the same:
  "adjudication pending" and "clean" look the same from the files;
* `promote`'s `StaleBatch` a round late is a symptom of the same thing.

**Implemented, staged:** `revision/_ledger.py` — one JSON line per
transition into `build/ledger.jsonl`, written by `build`, `promote` and
`baseline`, carrying the hashes. **Nothing reads it**, and
`test_nothing_READS_the_ledger_yet` enforces that by AST.

The staging is the point. A reader trusting the ledger today would meet
an absent record on its first real question — nine papers are mid-round
with nothing written. Write it for a release, read it in the next, and
that test is what the reader has to argue with rather than a promise
nobody remembers making.

## 2. `_xml` is private by name and public by use

| | |
|---|---|
| modules importing it | 41 of 58 (`errors` is next, at 32) |
| names in its `__all__` | 55 — the largest surface in the package |
| paper imports from it | 103: `visible_text` 75, `DOCUMENT` 52, `PARA_RE` 38, `RUN_RE` 7 |

The `api` gate now protects `_xml` as hard as anything, and the
underscore was a claim nobody honoured.

**Implemented:** those four are re-exported from `docxkit`. The module
keeps its name — 41 importers, and it IS internal-shaped. The papers
migrate as they are touched; `test_consumers.PRIVATE` records where they
still reach in, as a set that may only shrink.

## 3. The public surface is 7× what its consumers use

626 names in `__all__` across 35 modules. The papers use **128** (86
distinct spellings), 20 of them through a private path. With `api`
gating all 626, an internal rename cost a tag bump.

**Implemented:** `tools/consumers.py` writes the committed snapshot;
`api_check` reads it as the strict tier, resolved through griffe's
aliases — a paper imports `docxkit.tables.house` and griffe reports
`docxkit._table_core.house`, so matching the strings as written would
have put every facade name in the advisory tier.

Proved by breaking both ends: `edit.replace_in_para` losing a parameter
exits 1; `footnotes.orphans` losing one exits 0 and prints `unused`.

## 4. `cli.py` holds protocol decisions, not just rendering

2,357 lines; 36 `cmd_*` functions averaging 33 lines, but
`cmd_revision_validate` is 133 and `cmd_revision_ingest` over 100. The
lock/snapshot/drift decision fixed on 08-31 lived in
`cmd_revision_status`, not in `revision`. `cli.py` is held to 100 %
coverage — a policy that only makes sense because it *is* logic.

**Implemented for one command:** `revision.status(paper) ->
StatusReport`, with `drift_checked` named rather than inferred and
`exit_code` derived. 154 of `test_cli_revision`'s tests passed unchanged
across the move, which is what said it was behaviour-preserving.

**Do the rest per command as each is next touched, never batched.** Leave
`build_parser` alone: 407 lines of argparse declarations is a table, not
a function.

## 5. Prose is the architecture of memory, and prose has a half-life

38 % of the source is docstrings and comments, plus 15k lines of
markdown citing dates, papers, commits and measurements. For a
two-author codebase at 27 commits/day where every session starts cold,
this is the right bet.

The cost is the one the workflow note names about itself: **prose goes
stale and no test fails.** Two stale claims found in one session, both
load-bearing:

* `_xml`: "`P_RE` had the guard by accident of spelling ... the only walk
  that was right" — false for `<w:p w14:paraId="…"/>`, the form Word
  actually writes;
* `test_layering`: "`import docxkit` must not pay for lxml" — false, and
  ~9 ms.

Both sat beside tests asserting something adjacent.

**Convention, not yet enforced:** a claim about behaviour either points
at the test that holds it (`# gated: tests/test_import_cost.py`) or
carries its measurement date. And the long "what happened on paper X"
narratives belong in `BACKLOG-ARCHIVE.md`, leaving the rule and a
pointer — `tracked.build` is 334 lines, 71 of them docstring.

## 6. Tests that read source text

25 test files read a module's source and assert on its shape — "the
escape sentence appears twice", "`_build_args(r)` is called twice". Both
broke this week on legitimate refactors. They failed *correctly*, but
each is a test that fires on a rename rather than a behaviour change — a
sign the invariant could not be expressed in the design.

Where the invariant is "one declaration", make it one object and test
the output. Keep the ones guarding `tools/` — those are appropriate.

## 7. The sweep gate is red on a corrupt file

`SPI_Case-Ezhik-PC.docx` (2021, zip header, no central directory) made
the sweep exit 1 on every run that reached it — S3 by this repo's own
scale.

**Implemented:** `BadZipFile` on open is skipped, counted and named, on
the same line as the sweep count so "swept 300, skipped 1" and "swept 1,
skipped 300" cannot read alike. Only that exception: a `PermissionError`
or a raise inside docxkit is still a failure, and skipping `OSError`
would have swallowed the locked-file case the sweep exists to exercise.
Measured: 29 swept, 1 skipped, exit 0.

## Non-findings — measured, and not worth a batch

**`cli` fan-out 28, 65 lazy imports.** Right for 36 commands, and the
startup measures 43 ms.

**`tracked.build` at 263 code lines.** A single pipeline whose *order of
gates* is the design; splitting scatters it. If anything, lift the gate
sequence to data, as `tools/gates.py` does.

**The pinned `cli`↔`compare`↔`revision` cycle.** Deliberate,
function-level, tested.

**Dependency hygiene.** One hard dependency, extras declared, `deptry`
would be worth installing but nothing is currently wrong.

**vulture.** At 100 % confidence it produced one finding, and it is
wrong: it reads the `for…else: break` inside `while True:` in
`_table_core.py:615` as unreachable. At 60 % it emits 61, mostly
`__all__` exports. Bad signal-to-noise for a package that is mostly
public API.

**radon.** It flags `citations.repair_plan` at 32 where ruff's C901
passes it, because radon and mccabe count differently by construction.
Adopting it means re-tuning a threshold already measured and pinned in
`test_complexity_debt.py`.

**import-linter / tach.** `test_layering.py` already rejected
import-linter with a reason worth keeping — "the contract is small
enough to state as data, and a gate that needs no extra dependency is a
gate that actually runs" — and it covers layers, cycles and facade
halves.

**Three calling conventions.** `xml: str` (72 public functions), `parts`
(59), `Path` (46), `Paper` (12). Worth one paragraph in CONTRIBUTING,
because it is currently learned by reading; not worth unifying.

**`AnchorError(DocxKitError, AssertionError)`.** Odd, deliberate,
harmless.

## A note on one collision

`revision/__init__.py` does `from ._state import _state as _state`, so
`docxkit.revision._state` resolves to the FUNCTION, not the module —
found while probing this review. CONTRIBUTING names the module aliases
as the suite's test seam (`revision._validate`), and this is the one
half where that seam is shadowed. Nothing depends on it today. It is
recorded here rather than in BACKLOG because it has cost nothing yet.

## Test suite scale

2.2:1 test-to-source, 3k test lines/day. Correctness is fine;
navigation is not — `test_citations.py` is 4,348 lines,
`test_compare.py` 4,172. The 2026-08-15 review found one duplicated
block at a third of this size. Worth splitting the four files over 3k by
subject, and re-running `pylint --enable=duplicate-code` once after.
